Picking a school repaints uniforms, helmets, end zones, midfield logo, ribbon boards and 28,000 fans. Nothing loads; it rewrites a few color values.

Where the look comes from, and what it skips

ElementTypical AAA sports gameThis sample
PlayersScanned heads, 50k–100k triangle bodies, 4K texture sets per uniformAbout 230 triangles each, flat colors, glossy helmet shader. 22 players = 12 draw calls
AnimationThousands of motion-captured clipsProcedural strides, stances and a handful of arm poses computed per frame
UniformsUnique textures per team and per alternateA 7-color palette per team. ≈ 21 bytes a school
FieldHigh-res turf, normal and paint textures shipped on diskPainted into one 2048×1024 texture at load from yard math. 0 bytes shipped
StadiumHand-modeled venue per schoolGenerated from about a dozen numbers: bowl shape, row count, deck heights
CrowdAnimated 3D fans with several detail levels2-triangle cards in one instanced draw, bouncing in the vertex shader. Louder after big plays
Broadcast feelSame as hereNight lighting, camera framing, yellow first-down line, scorebug and banners. This is most of what reads as “Madden”

The browser version borrows three.js (about 600 KB) to talk to WebGL. The C++ build replaces it with the small OpenGL renderer from epic E3, so the scene code here is roughly what the game would ship, minus the library.
